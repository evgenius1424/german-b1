# Task: high-ROI vocabulary from one Goethe B1 Hören practice exam

You pick German words and expressions from the transcripts below that most improve a learner's chance of passing Goethe B1 Hören. This is not general vocabulary: an item earns its place when hearing and understanding it decides an answer.

## What to look for

Signal words that steer the meaning of a statement:

- corrections and contrasts: nicht … sondern, allerdings, stattdessen, doch, zwar … aber
- time and sequence: erst, schon, inzwischen, spätestens, nachher, vorher
- reasons and consequences: deshalb, trotzdem, obwohl, dafür
- conditions and restrictions: falls, nur, außer, mindestens, höchstens, kaum
- changes and cancellations: verschieben, absagen, ausfallen, stattfinden, nicht mehr
- rules, instructions and obligations: dürfen, erforderlich, abgeben, behalten
- answer-bearing concepts that recur across exam situations: enthalten, gelten, dauern, pünktlich, kostenlos, zusätzlich, Schicht

These are examples, not a list. Discover what this exam actually uses. Questions and answer keys are given where available: words in the evidence or the decisive distinction between answer and distractors are the strongest candidates.

## What to leave out

- proper names, people, cities, streets, brands, dates, times, numbers, weekdays
- basic A1 words (gehen, gut, Haus, heute, aber on its own, dann)
- niche nouns tied to this one scenario (Brauhaus, Domplatz, Helferzelt, Infostand)
- several synonyms doing the same job: keep the most useful one

## Form

- `expression`: dictionary form a learner should memorise. Verbs in the infinitive (separable verbs joined: ablaufen), nouns singular with article omitted, multiword units kept whole ("nicht mehr", "zwar … aber", "nicht … sondern"). Use "…" for a gap.
- `surface`: the exact fragment(s) as they appear in the transcript, in order, copied character for character. One string for a contiguous phrase ("nicht mehr", "abläuft", "gilt"); several for split forms (["läuft", "ab"], ["nicht", "sondern"]).
- `english`: short contextual translation, at most six words, the sense used here.
- `category`: one of $categories.
- `example`: one sentence copied exactly from the transcript that shows the expression, or "" if none is instructive. Keep it under 140 characters.
- `trap_note`: one short English sentence explaining how a learner could mishear or misread the expression in that example ("Here, erst means not until Wednesday, not first."), or "".
- Scores 0–10, judged honestly:
  - `answer_relevance`: hearing it changes the interpretation or identifies the correct answer
  - `cross_scenario`: useful across many B1 Hören situations (announcements, voicemails, talks, discussions)
  - `b1_likelihood`: likely to appear in B1 listening texts
  - `learner_confusion`: A2/B1 learners often misunderstand or overlook it
  - `context_clarity`: the transcript context makes the meaning clear

Frequency in this transcript is not evidence of value. Use the full scale: a 9 or 10 is rare and earned, a typical good candidate sits around 6–8.

Draw from all parts of the exam, not mainly one text, and mix signal words with answer-bearing concepts.
